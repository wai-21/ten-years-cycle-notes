# 01. Double Mismatch: The Slow Suicide of "Time Doesn't Match, Currency Doesn't Match"

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Match the maturity and the currency of every debt to the asset it funds.** Don't hunt for a magic third asset to "block" the risk — the problem was never which asset you hold, it's the structure itself.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- The 1997 Asian enterprise died of two independent misalignments firing at once:
  - **Maturity mismatch** — rolling 3-month loans to fund assets that take 5 years to pay back. A perfectly profitable business dies halfway because it "temporarily can't produce the lump sum."
  - **Currency mismatch** — revenue in baht, debt in dollars. When the local currency falls, the debt principal balloons on the books and eats every cent of profit.
- **The real detonation sequence:** a lender refuses to roll over (maturity leg snaps) → firms scramble for dollars (currency leg snaps) → both mismatches execute in the same week.
- **Gold is not the antidote.** Gold yields 0%: borrowing at 1% to hold it means rigid interest payments with no cash inflow — and gold is dollar-priced, so you've upgraded a currency bet into a currency-plus-commodity double exposure.
- **The interest spread is an insurance premium, not free money.** The extra yield on "borrow low-interest foreign currency, deposit high-interest local currency" is the market's price for bearing your devaluation risk and your rollover risk. You collect the premium; you pledge the principal.
- **The only long-term money without rigid constraints is equity** — no repayment date, no fixed coupon, and losses are shared. That is the structural cure for the double mismatch.

> *"Never fund illiquid core productive assets with short-term collateralized debt that can be marked to market at any moment."*
> *"East Asia has no spare tire."* — Asia leaned on short-term bank credit and never built a deep long-term equity market as a buffer.

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** I read "borrow abroad at 1.8%, earn 6% at home" as financial genius, and wondered whether gold — owned by no nation, forever valuable — could sit in the middle as an insulator.
- **After:** cash-flow conservation slapped it down. Zero-yield asset, rigid liability, and a 15% gold dip becomes a double hit. The real shift: **stop asking "which asset blocks the risk" and start asking "are maturities and currencies aligned."**
- **The harder layer:** the rivals funding long-term expansion with 3-month debt have already entered Minsky's speculative/Ponzi zone. Their party is not a competitive threat — it's the prelude to their liquidation. The strategy is to deleverage early, hold liquidity, and buy the wreckage cheap after they clear.
