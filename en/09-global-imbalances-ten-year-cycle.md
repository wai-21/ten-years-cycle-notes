# 09. Global Imbalances and the Ten-Year Cycle: How Defensive Hoarding Became the Next Tsunami's Fuel

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Know whether you are the creditor or the hostage — before you try to jump off.** Ask: are my "assets" someone else's liabilities? Is my "income" dependent on someone else's demand? Two yeses, and jumping is self-harm.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The reserve country is a hostage that looks like a creditor.** Ship real goods, receive foreign currency, lend it back at low rates (buy Treasuries). Stop lending and your customer can't buy your goods; to keep factories running and reserve values stable, you keep transfusing. **The more you produce, the more you lend, the less you dare leave.**
- **Jumping is self-harm twice over:** you are the largest holder — your own selling collapses the price and vaporizes what you haven't unloaded yet; and everyone converting back crashes your currency upward, tripling your export prices and zeroing your orders.
- **The Greenspan conundrum:** central banks hiked, but long rates stayed low — Asian savings kept flowing back. Facing cheap, endless liquidity, the financial system's reflex was: quality borrowers are maxed out, risk-free yields are crushed — **so lend to borrowers with no repayment capacity to speculate on housing (subprime), package it as "safe yield," and sell it back to the world.**
- **The Triffin dilemma:** the dollar is both a sovereign and a world currency. America must run deficits to supply global liquidity, which corrodes the dollar's credit long-term. **The knot cannot be solved, only moved.**
- **The full ten-year loop:**

```text
1997 Asian crisis (rescued on humiliating terms)
  ↓
Collective PTSD across Asia
  ↓
Mercantilist turn: suppress currencies, export hard,
hoard trillions in reserves (build the flood wall)
  ↓
Reserves can't be absorbed at home → flow back into Treasuries
  ↓
Global long rates crushed → Wall Street gets endless cheap funding
  ↓
CDOs, CDS, subprime → housing bubble
  ↓
2008 global crisis (the storm returns to the core, tenfold)
```

> *"Great powers unwilling to share power leave everyone to pray for themselves."* — Sheng on the collapse of crisis-coordination mechanisms.
> *"The maker becomes the hostage of his own reserves: don't lend, and the economy suffocates now; lend, and your sweat pays for someone else's bubble."*

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** in the industrial-park wargame I caught the first layer — "if token redemption breaks, the industrialist loses everything" — the reserve holder's fragility.
- **After:** pushed two layers down. First, **the reserve holder is the hostage who looks most powerful** — nominal top creditor, real holder of someone else's debt, dependent on someone else's demand. Second, **jumping off is self-harm**: I added the chain myself (reserves shrink → everyone dumps the token → asset prices spike → factory costs explode) and was pushed further — the dumping itself free-falls on the dumper, and the currency spike strangles exports.
- **The deepest layer became the spine of this whole archive:** I kept pulling the thread "how did 1997 beget 2008" until it gave the book its title. **A crisis is not a periodic accident; it is the byproduct of the last defense.** That changed how I judge any risk response — not "did it stop the bleeding," but "what risk did it defer, to whom, at what multiple?"
