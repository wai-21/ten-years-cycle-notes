# 10. Crony Capitalism and the East Asian Model: Turning "Equity Investment" into "Debt Chains"

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Capital that doesn't demand repayment demands control — you cannot have both.** "I want the equity market's money AND paternal autocracy" is a contradiction at the contract level; forcing it degenerates into disguised debt.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **Bank-dominated indirect finance = all social risk in one bucket.** Firms dislike diluting ownership; equity and bond markets stayed shallow; development money came almost entirely from bank loans. Society's commercial risk was never dispersed to equity investors — it all pooled as loans on bank balance sheets. External shock → profits fall → loans sour → bank capital wiped → runs.
- **Why firms resist equity: control plus leverage.** Equity means sharing future profits and some voice, in exchange for a cushion with no mandatory repayment. Debt means keeping absolute control while staking survival on "external cash flow and rollover never interrupt." When every entrepreneur in a country makes the same choice, development capital rests on a single pillar — the banking system.
- **Relationship lending distorts the price of capital:** allocation follows collateral (land, buildings) and political connections, not return on invested capital. Without deep markets, the economy loses its objective bankruptcy filter and risk-pricing ruler.
- **The institutional wound of crony capitalism:**
  - **Losses fully externalized:** politically sheltered groups get cheap, unlimited credit; downside goes to the public and depositors, upside (equity appreciation, land monetization, offshore transfers) consolidates as family wealth.
  - **Collateral value is symbiotic with political power:** the defaulted oligarch loans were all perfectly documented at origination — full appraisals, local-government endorsements. **When the political power collapses, recovery rates on such collateral are routinely under 10%.**
  - **Formal compliance is the camouflage of plunder.**
- **The benign exit: trade governance rights (voice) for risk-sharing capital (equity).** Investors accept unlimited downside only for transparent audits, board seats, related-party vetoes, and the power to replace management on operational default.

> *"Equity is the elastic shock absorber; bank credit is the rigid liquidation time bomb."*
> *"East Asia's deepest tumor: turning 'equity investment' into 'debt chains'."*

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** in the mold-factory wargame, my first instinct was standard worker-think — pledge the hardest, highest-book-value asset (50M of equipment).
- **After:** the asset-specificity discount destroyed it. Custom semiconductor tooling has almost no secondary buyers; once the market knows you're being liquidated, dealers quote scrap — 20–30% of cost. 50M recovers maybe 15M, **and you've severed your own future order book**. My second plan (stable-yield debt packages / sale-leaseback) was the same rigid claim in a costume. My third — non-voting equity — was rejected hardest: in an opaque family firm, a shareholder with no vote and no board seat is a sitting duck; the deal would inevitably rot into **named-equity-real-debt** — side letters, ratchets, and family personal guarantees.
- **The landing:** voting rights with teeth — board seats, related-party transaction veto, investor power to impeach management on operational default. That is the coming-of-age ceremony from feudal family shop to modern partnership.
- **The rule I keep:** payback period > 1 year plus uncertain cash flow → rigid-claim instruments are banned. Want patient equity → concede governance. Asset specificity high → never use it as a lifeboat.
