# 02. Liquidity vs Solvency: Who Gets Rescued, Who Must Die

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Ask "who takes the first loss" before "who rescues."** Rescue is not the endpoint — it eventually becomes taxes, inflation, or cut public services; rescuing without recognizing losses socializes private bad debt.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **Two entirely different diseases:**
  - **Illiquid (小李):** owns a $3M flat with zero debt; just can't produce $30K cash for a hospital deposit right now. Lending to him is a real rescue — the money comes back.
  - **Insolvent (小张):** owes $1M; total assets are a crashed car worth $100K. Lending to him doesn't save him — it delays the collapse and pulls your money into the hole.
- **The physics of banking:** depositors put in 100, the bank lends 90 to factories due in 3–10 years, and keeps 10 in the till. One rumor, everyone queues, and after the 10 is gone it's over — **a folder of excellent factory IOUs cannot become banknotes in time.**
- **The only cure for a run is to kill the panic:** not "please queue" (which signals scarcity and accelerates the stampede) but "government guarantees all deposits, cash supply unlimited, not one cent missing." Some banks physically stacked bales of banknotes behind glass so everyone could see them.
- **But this move requires a printing press — for your own currency.** Central banks cannot print dollars. If the debt is foreign-currency, reserves are finite, and "owing more dollars than you hold" ends in sovereign default.
- **The three-step bad-loan operation:** ① a government AMC / bad bank forcibly strips out the dead loans; ② auction them at fire-sale prices so the loss is **fully recognized on the books**; ③ recapitalize the original bank with clean capital. (Thailand's FRA in 1997, China's four AMCs in 1999.)
- **Moral hazard is the side effect:** every bailout must be paired with a penalty mechanism — clawbacks, personal-asset pursuit, equity wiped to zero — or next time someone bets bigger.

> *"Liquidity runs can destroy a seemingly healthy bank in an instant."*
> *"Losses must be recognized — denial guarantees failure."*

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** on "should we rescue an insolvent bank," I reached for an authority — central bank, bankruptcy administrator. I thought putting bad debt into a designated trash-can institution solved it.
- **After:** when pushed on "where does the hole finally land," my own answer — "everyone, through austerity" — rewrote my decision order: **first-loss allocation before rescuer selection.**
- **The third layer:** rescues must be synchronized with loss recognition. Rescue-without-recognition is "extend and pretend" — the nation's credit flows to zombie firms while healthy ones starve, ending in deflation and stagnation. (This is exactly why 1990s Japanese banks refused step one — haircutting principal — personal career risk plus the capital-adequacy red line.)
