# 08. The 1998 Hong Kong Battle: Change the Rules, Don't Outspend the Speculator

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Attack the attacker's daily cost of carry and his settlement deadline — not his wallet.** If your opponent is borrowing time, make time expensive.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The short sellers' two-market pincer:**
  1. **The quiet flank:** pile up massive Hang Seng Index futures shorts in advance, cheaply.
  2. **The loud feint:** dump HK dollars in the FX market, forcing the HKMA to defend the peg with rate hikes — the "seven wounds fist."
  3. **The harvest:** sky-high rates sink stocks as predicted; even at break-even in FX, the index futures shorts pay out billions.
  - **Corollary:** "defending the exchange rate" is the pre-scripted move. Fighting over dollar balances in the FX market is a frame you lose by entering.
- **August 1998 — the doctrine-breaking strike:** Hong Kong abandoned decades of "positive non-interventionism" and bought stocks directly, deploying about **HK$118 billion** of reserves to hold the Hang Seng above ~7,800.
- **The real kill was rule changes, not money:**
  - Raise margin requirements — leverage flips from weapon to liability within hours.
  - Enforce **T+2 physical delivery**, ban naked shorting; miss delivery and the clearing house buys at the day's highest price, charging you.
  - Lock up every lendable share — the defense buys and vaults the entire lendable supply, so shorts must bid against each other: a **short squeeze**.
- **The result and the bill:** on settlement day, 28 August 1998, the HSI closed at **7,829** on a record **HK$79 billion** turnover. The funds — already burning by the Russia default — cut and ran.
  - **The cost was credibility:** Friedman and others attacked "the referee playing striker." Hong Kong had to build the **Tracker Fund** to return the shares passively over years.
- **The external black swan that ended it:** Russia's GKO default on 17 August 1998 blew up LTCM and friends; global funds recalled capital home, and Hong Kong shorts faced margin calls from their own parent funds.

> *"A short's fatal weak point = leverage carry cost (margin) × physical delivery deadline (T+2). When the borrowed asset is bought up and vaulted by sovereign hands, every short sale is a donation of cheap ammunition to the defense."*

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** my answer was `1C / 2D` — "compare cash reserves; whoever bleeds out first loses." I read the battle as attrition.
- **After:** the chips were never symmetric. Hedge funds play with high-leverage borrowed money — win billions, lose a stopped-out retreat; the government stakes decades of public savings. And no single economy's reserves are truly "unlimited" against global investment banks over a six-month war. So the frame itself was wrong: **fight daily carry costs and settlement mechanics, not balance-sheet size.**
- **The two bad answers I offered and lost:** "capital admission gates" (blocks pension funds and exporters, not the funds already positioned inside) and "raise margins" alone (right direction, insufficient without T+2, the naked-short ban, and locking the borrow supply).
- **Two meta-rules that stayed:** hold physical control of your own chips — never sign default clauses letting custodians lend out your assets to short you. And any extraordinary intervention gets its **exit mechanism written on day one** (the Tracker Fund's passive unwind is the template).
