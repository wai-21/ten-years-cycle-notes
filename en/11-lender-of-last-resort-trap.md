# 11. The Lender-of-Last-Resort Trap: When the Savior Is Also the Scavenger

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Replace moral solidarity with priceable contracts.** Under extreme power asymmetry, a solidarity coalition without a liquidity moat gets picked off one member at a time.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The fatal misdiagnosis:** Korea and Thailand ran fiscal surpluses and high savings. Their disease was a **liquidity cardiac arrest** — short-term foreign debt maturing at once against thin reserves. The IMF mechanically applied the Latin-American playbook and treated it as a **chronic solvency disease**: rates to 30–80%, extreme fiscal austerity, forced closure of financial institutions.
  - The result: confidence never returned; healthy firms bled out indiscriminately; foreign capital harvested core Asian assets at fire-sale prices.
- **The rescuer's dual identity:** nominally the lender of last resort; functionally the **asset scavenger** representing creditor interests. "Shock therapy" was not designed for the patient's recovery — it was designed to monetize the patient's salable hard assets and lock in the creditors' exit before death.
- **Asymmetric hunting:** rescue packages came conditioned on scrapping foreign-ownership caps — buying the drowned man's assets while throwing the rope.
- **Capital controls are not backwardness:** Japan's Ministry of Finance tried to organize an **Asian Monetary Fund** in 1997 — Asians funding their own rollovers. It died instantly under the US threat "join the AMF, lose dollar-clearing access." Malaysia, by contrast, imposed controls and refused the prescription — the book's chapter is literally titled "Malaysia: Standing Alone."
- **The bottom line of any restructuring: protect the organs that generate blood.** Core R&D, own brands, key people — the last inch of the negotiating line. **Trade them for short-term cash and every subsequent rescue is meaningless.**
- **Restructuring tools that work:**
  - **Contingent debt-to-equity conversion:** 100% of equity pledged to an independent escrow; hit milestones and the pledge releases on a schedule; miss and conversion steps down a pre-set ladder — smoothly, no cliff.
  - **Collective action clauses (CAC):** 75% of creditors bind the rest; never privately sweeten a holdout.
  - **Residual-upside compensation:** haircut principal 30% and attach warrants on future upside — creditors become venture investors in the turnaround, not victims of it.
  - **Skin in the game:** management's equity and fees go to zero first, subordinated to everyone.

> *"Historically, roughly once a decade, a financial crisis strikes the world."* — Andrew Sheng, opening line of the book.
> *"I can give you more of the future's upside — but the principal must be extended now. You will not amputate my core limbs."* — my own table line from this wargame.

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** facing the "IMF in a suit" sell-on contract (cash for closing 25 of 40 stores, firing the R&D center, halving salaries, surrendering a veto plus 51% control and a 10-cent option on all overseas IP), my instinct was **moral solidarity** — rally local suppliers and staff into a "regional self-rescue community," bring in local capital to offset the foreign investor.
- **After:** one historical fact punctured it — Japan's AMF was strangled in an afternoon. With asymmetric power, **any solidarity bloc without a hard liquidity moat gets bought out**: the other side privately calls your two biggest suppliers and offers to cash out their claims if they force your liquidation on Wednesday. The alliance dissolves overnight.
- **The move that survived:** swap the bottom card from "shared future vision" to **priceable, executable contract weapons** — the contingent debt-to-equity pledge directly seals the counterparty's real fear (that you'll default or spirit assets away), trading a look-dutch option on control for time.
- **The two counter-traps I hadn't priced:** malicious default-induction (the investor engineers your missed KPIs to trigger the conversion at zero cost) and death-spiral convertibles (no floor price → every small miss dilutes harder → founders under 1%). Hence the pre-signed anti-sabotage covenant and floor price.
