# 13. From Crisis to Integration: The Chained Ships of Chibi — Why the Math of "Diversification" Sank Everyone Together

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Ask "can my guarantor fall at the same time I do?"** Correlation is not a physical property of assets — it jumps toward 1 in a crisis. A valid contract is not transferred risk.

## 📖 What the book actually says

**Three original sins, one causal chain.**

**Sin 1 — the Gaussian copula's mathematical arrogance.**
- Engineers flattened fat tails into normal distributions for tractability, **deleting the extreme events that actually occur**. Mathematically impeccable; wrong at the premise.
- Correlation was hard-coded as a constant ("hotpot and bubble tea: 0.05"). But **correlation is not a property of the asset — it is a function of the liquidity state**: independent in calm; at the crisis singularity, every institution sells for survival simultaneously and **everything correlates at 1.0**.
- The model didn't destroy risk — it became its transmission mechanism, delivering scattered local sparks into one powder magazine, on schedule and to spec.

**Sin 2 — integration dismantled the firebreaks.**
- Capital controls, FX friction, cross-border settlement delays — mocked as "inefficiency," they were in fact the real economy's **firebreaks**.
- Standardization, seamless clearing and financial freedom chained every balance sheet into one network topology — Cao Cao's ships bound together at Chibi: smooth as land in peace, total fleet loss from one hull's fire in war. **The price of zero friction is zero damping.**

**Sin 3 — originate-to-distribute destroyed prudence.**
- The old banker's floor: bad loans on loans you made are **yours**. Securitize and the originator keeps fees while the slices sell worldwide. **When decision-makers don't bear the downside of their own decisions, the system mass-produces junk** — not moral decay, incentive design. You get what you measure.

> *"In finance, correlation is not a physical property of assets but a function of the liquidity state."*
> *"The pursuit of zero-friction efficiency is paid for in zero damping of crises."*
> *"When lenders no longer carry the defaults, 'prudence' disappears from the dictionary."*

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **My picks in this wargame — `1B / 2A / 3B` — reproduced 2008 exactly:** trusting normal-distribution correlations (1B), stacking a synthetic CDO² on top of a failing pool to extend the debt chain (2A), and the zero-friction network-wide resonance (3B). The rating agencies stamped AAA on the re-cooked stew because the copula only audited the slice's variance, never whether the hotpot below had already stopped paying wages.
- **What actually killed the market in 2008 wasn't the defaults — it was not knowing:** when a European pension fund found real bankrupt hotpot shops inside its AAA paper, every institution simultaneously realized nobody knew whose balance sheet held how much of this. Interbank lending froze in 48 hours. **The liquidity black hole, not the losses, was the detonation.**
- **The judgment I keep:** if an asset's "safety" depends on something staying constant, the right question isn't "with what probability does it change" but **"what would make it change, and who else depends on the same constancy."** And before accepting any guarantee: **the guarantor's solvency and my survival must not share a failure mode.**
